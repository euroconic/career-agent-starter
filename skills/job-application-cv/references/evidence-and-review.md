# Evidence and review

For material claims, record the source, actual employer or engagement, timeframe, personal contribution and remaining caveats. Keep source observations separate from proposed wording.

For numbers, preserve the value, unit, baseline, denominator and period when known. Do not confuse documents with users, attendance with unique people, team output with individual ownership, capacity with realized savings, or targets with measured outcomes. Never sum overlapping cohorts. A percentage improvement in one task is not that percentage of all working time.

Delivery, learning, adoption and sustained outcomes are separate claims. Designing a workshop is not delivering it. A prototype is not a production deployment. Customer feedback is not a measured retention improvement. Participation is not ownership.

Conflicting dates, titles and employment relationships require evidence or a focused question. Preserve each source's attribution while unresolved. A template is design authority, not factual authority.

A review should explain what the CV proves, which screening objections matter, which issues concern wording versus missing evidence, and the smallest truthful improvements. Do not claim knowledge of an employer's internal ATS or hidden preferences.
